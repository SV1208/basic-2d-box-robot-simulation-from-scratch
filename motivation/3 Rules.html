The 3 Rules

For Building a Real Robotics/AI Career

Rule 1

Build Systems That Can Fail, Not Demos That Look Smart

If your work cannot fail in 10 different ways, it's useless for a Robotics/AI career.

🛑 STOP

  • Polished notebooks
  • Pretty visualizations
  • "Works in one case" projects

✅ START

  • Simulations with noise
  • Controllers that oscillate
  • Policies that crash
  • Logs of failures

Enforcement

Every project must answer:

  • What breaks first?
  • Under what noise?
  • How does error grow?
  • What assumptions are fragile?
Puts you ahead of 90% of applicants
Rule 2

Never Learn a Tool Until You Can Re-Implement It

Tools are not skills. Understanding is.

What This Kills

  • "I know ROS"
  • "I know PyTorch"
  • "I know SLAM libraries"
These are replaceable. AI already replaces them.

Before Using Any Library

  • Write a broken version yourself
  • Explain why it's broken
  • Explain what the library fixes

Examples

  • Own PID before control libs
  • Own Q-learning before RL frameworks
  • Own sensor model before perception

Test

Can't explain internals on whiteboard = You don't know it

AI replaces vs AI assists
Rule 3

Optimize for Irreversible Progress, Not Consistency

Consistency is a lie. Irreversible progress is real.

Reality

You will:

  • Miss days
  • Lose discipline
  • Fall into distractions
Trying to "never fail" is why people quit.

What Actually Works

Every week: create something that permanently raises your floor.

Examples

  • Reusable simulation framework
  • Controller you truly understand
  • Mental model of failure modes
  • End-to-end system document

Even after 10 days away, that knowledge doesn't reset.

Weekly Question

  • "If I vanished for a month, what would I still have?"

Answer = "nothing" → You were busy, not building
